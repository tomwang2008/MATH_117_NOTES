# Union and Intersection
> [!example] Definition of collection of sets
> Let $A$ and $\Omega$ be sets, and suppose that with each element $\alpha$ of $A$ there is associanted a subset of $\Omega$ which we denote by $E_{\alpha}$.
> 
> The set whose elements are the sets $E_{\alpha}$  will be denoted by $\{ E_{\alpha} \}$. Instead of speakingof sets of sets, we shall sometimes speak of a collection of sets, or a family of sets.

> [!example] Definition of Union
> The union of the sets $E_{\alpha}$ is defined to be the set $S$ such that $x \in S$ if and only if $x \in E_{\alpha}$ for at least one $\alpha \in A$. We use the notation
> $$
> S= \bigcup_{\alpha \in A}E_{\alpha}
> $$
> If A consists of the integers $1,2,\dots,n$ one usually writes
> $$
> S=\bigcup_{m=1}^nE_{m}
> $$
> If A is the set of all positive integers, the usual notation is 
> $$
> S=\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}E_{m}
> $$

> [!example] Definition of Intersection
> The intersection of the sets $E_{\alpha}$ is defined to be the set $P$ such that $x \in P$ if and only if $x \in E_{\alpha}$ for every $\alpha \in A$. We use the notation 
> $$
> P=\bigcap_{\alpha \in A}E_{\alpha}
> $$
> or 
> $$
> P=\bigcap_{m=1}^{n}E_{m}
> $$
> or
> $$
> P=\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}E_{m}
> $$
