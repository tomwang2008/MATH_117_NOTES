# Metric Space
> [!example] Definition of metric space
> A [[Math/Principles of Mathematical Analysis/Set\|set]] $X$, whose elements we shall call points, is said to be a metric space if with any two points $p$ and $q$ of $X$ there is associated a real number $d(p,q)$, called the distance from $p$ to $q$, such that
> - (a) $d(p,q)>0$ if $p\neq q$; $d(p,q)=0$;
> - (b) $d(p,q)=d(q,p)$
> - (c) $d(p.q)\leq d(p,r)+d(r,q)$, for any $r\in X$.
>   Any function with these three properties is called a distance function, or a metric
